Session Prep — The Faerie Wood: What Kreth Left

Full lore: The Faeries of Thistleglass — the Eyes of Kreth, the glamour, the moral problem.
Cosmology: Author's Notes → Why the gods left.
Fae stat blocks: Encounter tracker — for a flashpoint, not the spine.

An investigation, not a fight. The wood's eye-bearers hold pieces of the truth and cannot read them, because the Eyes of Kreth show them the world as it should be. The party has two clearer lenses: their own outside eyes (with Draz's Splendor-sight and Sylvie's Athenaeum reading), and the crone — a human, Umbra-deformed but never eye-bearing, who still sees the wood as it is.

Three things are true here for the party to work out. Each has three clues, found in different ways and different places.

The hook

  • Speaker now wears the Eyes of Kreth and has spoken once. Where did the eyes come from, what is their covenant, what did they do to it?
  • Sylvie's question rides under everything: why did the gods leave, and would they resent being called back? This is the wood of a god who left — and there is a beckoning in her own chest she has started to notice.

What is true (GM)

  • Kreth meant it. When he left, he indicated he would return — and he was not lying. He does not know why he left (the gods leave on a blind migratory impulse — see Author's Notes), but the intent to come back is sincere, even from the god of deception. He left his eyes behind: a god does not leave his eyes unless he means to return for them. The Eyes of Kreth are a pledge as much as a remnant.
  • Splendor has its own gravity, and it points up and out. It is the pull that drew the gods away. Sylvie, brimming with Splendor since the Grithmaar geyser, has begun to feel the same beckoning — not a thought, a weight, always leaning skyward, out of the world.
  • Splendor is positivity-dominant. The Umbra contaminates like a drop of filth in clean water; Splendor is the reverse — a little of it transforms a lot of Umbra. The world is not doomed by the dark if even a little light returns.
  • The crone. Human, at least mostly; twisted by the Umbra but never gouged for the Eyes of Kreth, so she keeps her own senses. She lives in a hutch at the edge, sees the wood as bog and rot where the fae see a glade, and remembers Kreth's leaving with her own eyes. Cryptic and transactional, but the one clear witness in the wood.

Revelations & clues — three each, mixed method and place

Keep discovery cheap (a 10–12 clears any roll); at least one clue per revelation needs no roll; never gate a conclusion behind a single find.

Revelation 1 — Kreth meant it: his intention to return is sincere

  • The crone (social, hutch): she watched him go with her own eyes, not the eye-bearers' gilded memory. He gave a plain promise as he left, and she — no fool for glamours — believed it. Her account carries the weight the fae's cannot.
  • The departure site (active, physical): where Kreth left, his eyes still well up out of the ground. A god leaves his eyes only as surety — a thing you come back for. Reading it plainly (no glamour), the site is not an abandonment; it is a pledge with collateral.
  • Speaker / Splendor-read (lateral): Speaker, now carrying Kreth's eyes, or Sylvie reading the residue of the leaving, finds no deceit in it. The moon-god is the god of lies, and the party's instinct is to distrust — but this one act is clean.

Revelation 2 — Splendor's gravity pulls up and out, and Sylvie feels it

  • Sylvie's own body (no roll, experiential): the more Splendor she holds, the stronger the beckoning — up, out, away from the world. Not a temptation of the mind; a gravity. Let it grow across the session.
  • The crone (social, hutch): she names it. She has seen it before — in the gods, in eye-bearers who drank too much light, in anyone who fills with Splendor. "It wants to go home, and home is up." She marks it in Sylvie and warns her.
  • The wood's Splendor (active/observed): to Draz's sight every mote of Splendor in the wood leans the same way — skyward, outward, like filings to a pole. The eye-bearers' brightest strain up. The gravity is visible if you can see the light.

Revelation 3 — Splendor is positivity-dominant: a little transforms a lot

  • A transformed patch (active, physical): somewhere in the rot, a single true mote of Splendor has turned a wide blighted stretch genuinely lovely — not a glamour, a real transformation, out of all proportion to its size.
  • The crone herself (social, hutch): Umbra-deformed yet still herself, her senses intact, because a scrap of Splendor she keeps holds the dark at bay. She is the living proof; she can say it plainly — "a drop of the good undoes a flood of the bad."
  • Sylvie's own power (lateral, test): she has done it — at the Athenaeum she pulled the Umbra out of corrupted students with her light (S21). A touch of her Splendor on something blighted here transforms it far past what its size should allow.

The crone

  • What she is: human, Umbra-warped, un-eyed; keeps her own true senses. The fae tolerate or avoid her; she is not of their court and not blind to it.
  • What she knows: Kreth's leaving (she was there), the pull of Splendor (she has watched it take others), why she is still herself (the scrap of light). She does not know everything, and she will not simply tell — she trades, tests, and talks sideways.
  • How to run her: the clear lens against the fae's distortion. Where an eye-bearer cannot see the truth, the crone can, and chooses what to share. Naming, price, and disposition are yours.

Running it — hints when they stall

Deliver hints diegetically and proactively; a hint suggests a question, it does not answer it.

  • Speaker as a compass — when the party drifts, it turns or speaks a fragment toward the departure site.
  • Sylvie's body escalates — the beckoning sharpens on its own; she does not need to roll to feel it worsen.
  • The crone volunteers — if a thread goes cold, she offers a sideways hint for a price.
  • No dead ends — each revelation has one clue that needs no roll (the crone's word, Sylvie's body, the crone-as-proof).

The fae as unreliable lenses

  • Eye-bearers cannot perceive the truth — the eyes overwrite it in real time. Do not let them confess; let them fail to see.
  • The crone is the counter-lens — she sees what they can't, and withholds by choice, not blindness.

If it turns to a fight

Threatening the crone, or trying to take or cut out eyes, breaks the peace — then the Guard (S25 roster on the tracker). Combat is a consequence of clumsy investigation, not the plan.

Open calls

  • The crone's name, price, and disposition — ally, oracle, or hazard.
  • How far Sylvie's beckoning goes this session, and whether she can (or wants to) resist it.
  • Whether the party can act on Revelation 3 here — turn a piece of the wood, or the crone's lot, with Splendor.
  • Kreth's return: does the party get a sense of when, or only that?
  • Whether the wood yields a Kreth token / blessed remnant for the dormant revive-the-gods plan.
