Session Prep — Thistleglass, the False Glade

Thistleglass, the False Glade — Session 25 Prep

Encounter tracker for tonight: Thistleglass — the False Glade (live HP/Stress, features, art).
Full lore: The Faeries of Thistleglass (Behind the Veil) — the Eyes of Kreth, the glamour, the moral problem, and faerie-name examples.

The jump (opening)

Of the Amber Reach works Sylvie carried out of the Forbidden Library, one image sparks her, magically — a faerie glade seen through a horror fun-house mirror, beautiful and wrong at once. She can't place it; she believes it's one of the marked sites on the world-map. With the Athenaeum failing around them (pyre thin, dark breaking in, Inola gone), the image pulls at her, and she uses it as her true face and teleports the party into the glade. Their exit from the collapsing compound and their arrival are the same beat.

They don't know where they are. Piecing out that this is Thistleglass — the last of Ygva's god-leads, "Faerie's Guard," the domain of Kreth (False Visions / the Moon) — is a reveal to earn, not to hand over.

What the place is (Kreth's nature)

  • It presents as an impossibly lovely faerie glade — a glamour over something wrong. The fun-house mirror is literal: perception here lies. Distances, faces, the beautiful thing itself — all the trap. Under moonlight the glamour is strongest.
  • Kreth = False Visions, the Moon (false hope, paralysis of thought — the Moon Rune). The place shows you what you want and uses it against you.
  • The faeries chose this. When the Umbra fell, Thistleglass didn't resist it — it matched it. Every cruel fairy-tale (stolen children, impossible bargains, revels that swallow a hundred years) is literal truth here. They didn't become mindless revenants; they decided the world is beautiful, and for them it is. No Sacred Pyre, no enduring — a paradise built on a god's refusal to accept what's true.
  • The Eyes of Kreth. The god left behind divine eyeballs — organs of perception from the god of deception. A faerie who gouges out their mortal sight and fills the sockets with them becomes an eye-bearer: pale, luminous orbs like little moons staring from a blight-twisted face. Eye-bearers are the elite caste — priesthood and court both — who shape the glamours and enforce the bargains; the eyeless fae serve, follow, or fear them. The Eyes are limited, un-manufacturable divine remnants (kin to the Blessed Branches of Caranthalis and the Askavir of Tlellic). (The Guard's leaders read as eye-bearers — see the roster below.)
  • "Faerie's Guard" — the glade is warded by its fae, who fight with glamour rather than steel.

Why it pulls — this is Sylvie's arc

Ygva (who wanted the totems) is dead, so the external reason is thin — the pull is personal. This is her moon: she "called it back" (S17), she's "of the moon," her lost Stoneweaver crown is up there. Seed one or more:

  • False visions aimed at Sylvie — Kreth shows her what she's lost: her torn-out heart, her Stoneweaver kin, a version of herself never touched by Umbra. False hope, to lure or trap.
  • Kaelyn & Rhys converge here — the moon-tied Stoneweavers (Rhys undead) are the natural residents of Kreth's realm; this could be where they went.
  • The truth of "Sister Moon" — Kreth may hold why she is of the moon at all.

Draz's home ground

The other pull is Draz's — this is his country. Thistleglass sits in the southern coastal region he's from; he knows people and places here in a way no one else does. That gives him the session's second spine: he may recognize a landmark under the glamour, name a road, or be recognized himself.

  • Geography to have loose: the wood is tagged Forest / Mystical (the only Mystical settlement), roped by road to Redmorrow (a ruthless smuggler's haven built from grounded shipwrecks) and Salthewn (a coastal village burning black-pitch fires on buoys against sea evils). If the party wants out — or a mundane foothold — those are the doors. Redmorrow especially could be gateway or buffer between the fae and the wider world.
  • Draz's read on "home" is itself a glamour target: Kreth can show him the home he lost, or people who should be dead.

The moral problem

Hold this under everything: the question isn't only how do we fight this but whether the faeries are even suffering — whether breaking the glamour is rescue or cruelty. They've built a paradise out of a god's refusal to accept the truth. Some prefer the lie. Some may not know it is a lie. Let the party's instinct to "save" these people be complicated by fae who don't want saving.

The false-vision ruling

The glamour is real — for those inside it. This is the key ruling and the thing that makes Thistleglass different from ordinary illusion: when an eye-bearer reshapes the world, it is reshaped for anyone caught in it. If the fae decide the path leads to a warm hearth and a feast, the party walks, and eats, and feels full — while their bodies kneel in a bog, mouths full of mud and thorns, drowning comfortably and gratefully. The pain doesn't come through. The warmth is genuine.

  • You can't "disbelieve" it. There's no perceive-truly save that lifts the veil; the lie isn't in their heads, it's in the world around them. Don't offer a roll to "see through."
  • Breaking it is worse. Dispelling a glamour (a Splendor surge, bright light, killing the eye-bearer shaping it, Sylvie's Umbra) doesn't reveal a tidy truth underneath — it destroys the reality they're living in and drops them into whatever is actually happening to their bodies, mid-consequence. The player who forces the truth may wish they hadn't.
  • Play it as dread, not damage. Let a beautiful beat run long before the floor falls out. Under moonlight the glamour is strongest; a Splendor source or bright light is the party's lever to break it — at the cost above.
