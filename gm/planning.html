Session Prep — The Faerie Wood: What Kreth Left

Full lore: The Faeries of Thistleglass — the Eyes of Kreth, the glamour, the moral problem.
Cosmology: Author's Notes → Why the gods left.
Fae stat blocks: Encounter tracker — for a flashpoint, not the spine.

An investigation, not a fight. Clues are seeded across the wood; the fae hold every one of them and understand none of their import, because the Eyes of Kreth show them the world as it should be, never as it is. The party — outside the eyes, with Draz's Splendor-sight and Sylvie's Athenaeum reading — can read what the fae cannot.

The hook

Two pulls, both already in play:

  • Speaker now wears the Eyes of Kreth (set into it during the Mirror fight, S25) and has spoken once in a borrowed voice. What did the eyes do to it — where did they come from, what is their covenant, can they be understood or removed?
  • Sylvie's question rides under everything: why did the gods leave, and would they resent being called back? This is the wood of a god who left — and left a piece of himself in the ground.

The concrete goal the party can name: understand the Eyes of Kreth — their source and what Kreth's leaving actually was.

What is true (GM)

  • Kreth left on the same blind migratory impulse as every god — no reason, no choice, no knowledge of why. He did not leave to "fetch" anything. The fae's belief that he will return is a rationalization, and the eyes enforce it: an eye-bearer sees the world as it should be, so they cannot perceive the blank fact of abandonment. The Eyes of Kreth are that rationalization made flesh.
  • The eyes surface in exactly one place: the Unwatched Place — the scar where Kreth actually departed, the single spot in the wood the glamour cannot rewrite, because an absence cannot be dressed as a presence. Kreth's leavings — the divine organs of perception — well up there. The fae tend it as a shrine of waiting.
  • No eye-bearer can truly look at the Unwatched Place; the eyes edit it out. The eyeless fae half-know it and look away.

Revelations & clues — three clues each, three methods

Keep discovery cheap (a 10–12 clears any roll); every revelation has one clue that needs no roll at all; never gate a conclusion behind a single find.

Revelation A — The eyes come from the Unwatched Place, and the fae cannot see it

  • Active (search/travel): ask to be shown where eyes are given, and every fae's directions bend away from one part of the wood; walk it yourself and the glamour thins — the beautiful glade drops to bog and thorn around a scoured hollow.
  • Social (no roll): a young eyeless fae, or the Sweet One off-guard, calls it "where the light went out" or "the place he doesn't watch," then flinches from having said it.
  • Lateral (Splendor-sight / Speaker): everywhere else the eyes read to Draz as dense Splendor-motes (Kreth's remnants); the hollow reads as a Splendor scar — an absence shaped like a god. Speaker, bearing the eyes, turns toward it unbidden.

Revelation B — Kreth left for nothing; "he will return" is a story the eyes hold in place

  • Active (read the relic): at the hollow, a mark left in Kreth's own hand. Read plainly — without the eyes' gilding — it records only a leaving, unexplained: no errand, no promise. An eye-bearer reading the same mark recites a promise that is not written there.
  • Social (ask around — the contradiction): press several fae on what Kreth went to fetch and get different answers — a fallen star, a stolen child, the sun, his own lost eye. Confabulation, not memory; the story is generated fresh each time.
  • Lateral (Sylvie's research): Sylvie matches it to the God-King's last writ from the Athenaeum — the same after-the-fact apology dressing an inevitability as a choice. The pattern is a god's, not a fae's. (Ties to Why the gods left.)

Revelation C — This is what a god's leaving looks like from the inside (Sylvie)

The payoff for her godhood question, not a clue-hunt. At the scar she can feel the shape of it: not malice, not a decision — an emptying, a bird already gone before it knew it was leaving. Whatever she is becoming, this is the far end of the road. Let her draw her own conclusion; do not state the moral.

Running it — hints when they stall

Deliver hints diegetically and proactively; a hint suggests a question, it does not answer one.

  • Speaker as a compass: when the party drifts, it turns, or speaks a fragment, toward the Unwatched Place.
  • The scar pulls (as the large orb pulled Sylvie in S25): proximity, not a roll, draws them.
  • A fae's story cracks on its own if pressed twice — the seams of the glamour show without anyone rolling.
  • No dead ends: if they miss the relic, an eye-bearer recites the false promise in front of them and the contradiction with the eyeless fae's account does the work.

The fae as unreliable lenses

  • Eye-bearers cannot perceive the truth — the eyes overwrite it in real time. Do not let them "confess"; let them fail to see.
  • Eyeless fae half-know and deflect. Avoidance marks proximity to the truth; contradiction marks confabulation.

If it turns to a fight

Forcing an eye-bearer to the Unwatched Place, or trying to take or cut out eyes, breaks hospitality — then the Guard (S25 roster on the tracker). Combat is a consequence of clumsy investigation, not the plan.

Open calls

  • What Speaker bearing the eyes now means, and whether they can be removed without destroying it.
  • Kreth's own rationalization (see the Why the gods left thread) — does it surface here, and is it his alone or shared with the other gods?
  • What Sylvie takes from the scar toward — or away from — her reach for godhood.
  • Whether the wood yields a Kreth totem / blessed remnant for the dormant revive-the-gods plan.
